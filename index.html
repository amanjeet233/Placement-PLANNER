PHASE 1 · FOUNDATIONS Weeks 1–3 · 04 Sep – 24 Sep 2026

Phase 1 — Absolute Foundations & Logic

Comfort with Java syntax, logic-building via pattern printing and paper dry runs, foundational arithmetic formulas, daily MySQL from scratch, and confidence in interview delivery.

🧠 Logic Building (Do First)
  • Pattern printing: 15–20 patterns to build loops & nested index instinct.
  • Dry run on paper before writing code. Trace steps manually first.
  • Pure logic: Swap, prime check, palindrome, factorial, Fibonacci.
  • Golden Rule: Can't write the steps on paper? Don't code yet.
💻 Java + Coding Base
  • Java setup & syntax: Variables, primitive data types, operators, I/O.
  • Flow control: if-else, while, for loops, switch statements.
  • Methods & Arrays: Return types, parameters, 1D & 2D array traversals.
  • OOP fundamentals: Class, object, constructor, this keyword.
🧮 Aptitude (Arithmetic)
  • Number System: Divisibility rules, unit digits, LCM & HCF shortcuts.
  • Percentages: Fractional conversions, percentage change, word problems.
  • Commercial Math: Profit & Loss, Discount, Simple & Compound Interest.
  • Formula notebook: Maintain hand-written formulas for 1-3-7 revisions.
🗄️ MySQL (1 Hr/Day Parallel)
  • Environment: Install MySQL & Workbench / XAMPP, connect to localhost.
  • DQL & Filtering: SELECT, FROM, WHERE, ORDER BY, LIMIT, OFFSET.
  • DML & DDL: INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, CREATE TABLE, data types.
  • Operators: AND, OR, IN, NOT IN, BETWEEN, LIKE with wildcards.
🗣️ Spoken English & Interview
  • Daily 10-min recording: Speak on 1 question daily and review playback.
  • Filler-word tally: Replace "um", "like", "basically" with silent pauses.
  • 60-Second Self-Intro: Deliver clean introduction without notes.

🗣️ Interview Communication

Fixing Interview Speaking (Filler Words & Confidence)

⚠️ Catch These Filler Words
  • "like" — Used when stalling for thoughts.
  • "umm / uhh" — Habitual vocal hesitation.
  • "actually / basically" — Overused conversational crutches.
  • "you know" — Seeking validation from interviewer.
✅ Replace With Power Habits
  • Silent 1-second pause: Makes you look thoughtful and composed.
  • "Let me explain with an example": Instantly grounds abstract theory.
  • Short crisp sentences: End your sentence with a clear full-stop.

  • Record: Answer 1 question from the bank on phone camera.
  • Replay: Count exact filler words used and log the number.
  • Re-record: Speak again targeting fewer fillers and calmer posture.
🔂 05 · MEMORY RETENTION PROTOCOL

The 1-3-7 Revision Rule (Don't Let Anything You Learn Get Forgotten)

🧠
What It Is & Why It Works
Your brain forgets new information fast unless it's reviewed at increasing intervals. The 1-3-7 Rule means: revise what you learned 1 day later, again 3 days later, and again 7 days later. After the 3rd revision, it moves permanently into long-term memory — this is why "I studied it once but forgot in the interview" happens, and this rule directly fixes it.
1️⃣ Day 1 Revision

The next day after learning a topic (e.g., Binary Search), spend 10–15 min re-solving 2 problems from it without looking at notes first.

🎯 Catches what didn't actually stick
3️⃣ Day 3 Revision

3 days after learning, revisit the same topic again — this time try a slightly harder variation on it.

🧩 Confirms topic is sticking well
7️⃣ Day 7 Revision

7 days after learning, do a final quick revision — explain the topic out loud in your own words for 1 minute and solve 1 problem.

🗣️ Doubles as interview-delivery practice
📒
How to Track It — Simple Method
Keep a small notebook or notes app with 3 columns: Topic | Date Learned | Revision Dates (Day 1 / Day 3 / Day 7). Every night, check which topics are "due" for revision tomorrow and tick them off. This applies to DSA topics, aptitude formulas, and even interview answers — anything you want to remember under high-pressure interview settings.
🔥 06 · PSYCHOLOGY & CONSISTENCY

How to Stay Motivated for 90 Days

⚡
The Consistency Principle
Motivation naturally drops after the first 1–2 weeks for everyone — that's normal, not a sign you're failing. The plan below is built so you keep going even on low-motivation days.
🔗 Don't Break the Chain

Mark an ✗ on a calendar/app every single day you did any study, even 20 minutes. Your only goal is to never have 2 blank days in a row. The visual streak becomes its own motivation.

🎯 Small Daily Wins

Set one tiny, guaranteed-achievable goal per day ("solve 1 problem," "record 1 answer"). Hitting small targets daily builds momentum faster than chasing big, vague goals like "get better at DSA."

🚫 No Comparison

Don't compare Day 5 of your prep to someone else's Day 90. Compare only to your own last week — are you solving faster, speaking cleaner than 7 days ago? That's the only useful comparison.

🏆 Weekly Rewards

Every Sunday, if you hit the week's targets, give yourself a small real reward — a favorite meal, a movie, extra sleep. Motivation lasts longer when effort is actually celebrated, not just expected.

👥 Accountability Partner

Tell one friend/senior your weekly targets and report back to them every Sunday. Just knowing someone will ask "did you do it?" significantly increases follow-through.

📉 Low-Motivation Day Plan

On days you truly don't feel like it — don't skip completely. Do the smallest possible version (1 problem, 5-min speaking practice). Showing up beats intensity; it keeps the streak and habit alive.

🖼️ Visualize the Outcome

Spend 2 minutes each morning picturing the placement offer letter or the exam result you're working toward. This primes your brain to prioritize the day's study over distractions.

📊 Track Progress Visibly

Keep a simple counter: problems solved, mock interviews done, aptitude tests taken. Watching the numbers climb over 90 days is one of the strongest motivators — progress feels tangible when tracked.
